1 · The problem space

What “commodity derivatives intelligence” means here

Commodity derivatives intelligence turns raw exchange prints (settlements, volume, open interest, expiries) into an explanation of why prices differ and a decision about what to do.

MCX lists gold in five sizes. A gram of gold should cost the same in each, but it doesn’t, and the reasons stack up:

  • quotation unit and purity (995 vs 999);
  • expiry date (around the 5th of the month vs month-end, which means different carry);
  • which spot price each contract settles to;
  • delivery terms (bar vs coin, making charges, tender windows);
  • the cost and difficulty of trading small lots.

Intelligence means separating the fair parts of a gap from the part someone is overpaying, and only calling it an opportunity when it survives costs.

Who needs it

Retail and small investors

The fastest-growing group: bullion rose from 44% to 57% of MCX ADT (Q2 FY26 vs Q2 FY25, per MCX’s release) on the back of the mini variants. They choose which contract to hold, with no view of which is rich or cheap.

Who needs it

Prop and arbitrage desks

They need relative value that holds up after real costs, contract-keyed history, and tender-aware exits. Today that means paid feeds plus in-house spreadsheets.

Who needs it

Exchange and regulator

Is each variant fairly priced against its settlement reference? SEBI’s Feb 2026 circular made polled spot the ETF valuation benchmark, so fairness to that reference now has policy weight.

2 · Key findings

The small contracts carry a premium that only settlement removes

Our own probe of real MCX data, and the lead the scope is built around. GOLDGUINEA and GOLDPETAL settle to the same per-gram price as GOLDTEN, yet they traded above it for most of the contract’s life. G0 result: GO on both pairs. Across all 18 same-expiry cycles (Apr 2025 – Sep 2026), GOLDGUINEA trades at +79 bp and GOLDPETAL at +94 bp over GOLDTEN on VWAP, and every cycle is positive. GitHub · 01

11 teams already built the standard pipeline

  • Normalize → carry → z-score → walk-forward → “no edge” is now the baseline.
  • gold-rv is strongest: three years of real data, frozen-parameter test, t = 1.89, pitch deck.
  • Everyone assumes slippage. gold-rv’s break-even is 3.8 bp against an assumed 5 bp.
  • curl_cffi + bidask (EDGE spread estimator) are the two libraries that change our outcome.
Papers · 02

Contract design is a price component

  • Delivery options lower futures prices (Gay–Manaster 1984; Boyle 1989).
  • Limits to arbitrage explain persistent gaps (Shleifer–Vishny 1997).
  • Spot vs term premia justify our P&L split (Szymanowska et al. 2014).
  • Foundation models don’t beat HAR (Brini 2026), so we skip ML forecasting.
  • We found no paper on cross-size MCX gold pricing.
Patents · 03

No US-filing blockers; one useful contrast

Searched US filings on Google Patents only. Indian filings were not searched.

  • CME holds an active patent (US8255305B2, to 2030) on implied ratio spreads between mini and full-size contracts. It links sizes inside the matching engine. MCX has no such link, which may help explain the premium.
  • Z-score stat-arb filings were abandoned (prior art).
  • Avoid framing the product as auto-execution, chart-pattern alerts or a composite strategy score.
Landscape · 04

Big tools are generic; Indian apps show price only

  • Bloomberg CCRV/HS and LSEG CURVE/CFPD do curves and spreads, but know nothing of polled spot or coin delivery.
  • Dhan, Groww and Quantsapp show price, OI and expiry. Dhan’s “premium over spot” has no stated formula.
  • Gro Intelligence shut down in 2024: “AI insights” without a named decision-maker failed.

Data · 06

MCX is reachable, the right way

  • Plain HTTP gets an Akamai 403; Chrome-TLS impersonation gets 200.
  • mcxlib’s POST endpoints are dead. The new GET endpoints include a full-history call per contract, so the full backfill is about 400 requests.
  • A per-contract polled spot archive exists, the settlement reference nobody uses.
  • Holidays return empty data; dates come back MM/DD/YYYY; symbols are space-padded.

Gaps we can exploit
  • Settlement-reference blindness. Everyone compares futures with futures. We compare each contract with the price it actually settles to.
  • Specs treated as constant. gold-rv’s unexplained ~250 bp GOLDPETAL shift (Dec 2024–Mar 2025) lines up in time with the Dec 2024 GOLDPETAL circular (₹100/g making charge). That is a hypothesis. The charge is paid buyer → seller at delivery, on top of a DDR the futures price converges to anyway, so the price mechanism isn’t obvious. Tested in S1.
  • Assumed costs. A measured spread estimate can flip, or firm up, the “no edge” verdict.
  • No user. Every build is a terminal for arbitrageurs that concludes there is no arbitrage. A retail chooser stays useful whatever the verdict.
Wider market
  • Global terminals (Bloomberg, LSEG): curve and spread tools, priced for institutions, no MCX contract-design layer.
  • Alt-data (Kpler, Vortexa, ChAI): flows-driven intelligence for energy and metals. Gold isn’t shipping-driven.
  • Indian retail apps (Dhan, Groww, Zerodha, Quantsapp, Sensibull, Opstra): price, OI, options. No cross-contract fairness.
  • Data vendors (TrueData, Global Datafeeds): paid feeds. Our fallback if scraping breaks.

5 · Proposed hackathon scope

Tola: the fair-price desk for MCX gold

A tola is the traditional Indian gold weight, 11.66 g. The name is a placeholder.

The problem we solve

The same gram of gold costs a different amount in each MCX contract. Nobody tells the person buying which part of that gap is fair (purity, carry, settlement reference, delivery terms) and which part is a premium they pay for small lots. Tola decomposes every gap against the exchange’s own settlement references. It then tests whether the unexplained part can be traded after measured costs, and turns the result into a decision: which contract to hold, when to roll, when to stay out.

Primary user

Active retail and HNI gold traders

People choosing between GOLDM, GOLDTEN, GOLDGUINEA and GOLDPETAL to express a gold view or accumulate grams. The decision: which contract and expiry is cheapest to hold for N days.

Secondary user

Prop relative-value desks

Small-lot premium signals with honest, cost-measured backtests and quiet-by-default alerts.

Impact angle

Exchange and SEBI

A public “contract fairness” view: how far each variant trades from its settlement reference, and whether retail pays for small lots.

How a price gap breaks down

1 · UNITPer fine gramDivide by quote grams × purity, using the dated spec version.
2 · CARRYExpiry gapImplied carry from each contract’s own curve (5th vs month-end).
3 · REFERENCESettlement spotAhmedabad vs Mumbai poll; single poll vs 3-day average. Measured from polled-spot data.
4 · DELIVERYContract termsBar vs coin, tender window and margin, making charge. Annotated, not subtracted: whether these move pre-expiry prices is a hypothesis (S1).
5 · RESIDUALSmall-lot premiumWhat’s left. Measured at +79 to +94 bp (G0: GO), then tested for tradability in M4.

Steps 1–2 are what every competitor does. Steps 3–5 are new, and each uses exchange data or spec documents we verified. The ₹100/g making charge goes into the chooser’s take-delivery cost either way.

G0 · Premium gate: go/no-go by Fri 16 Oct

If the premium is a Close-timing artefact, the headline goes. So we test it before building on it. The rule was frozen in g0_gate/G0_PREREGISTRATION.md before the backfill ran.

  • Backfill: every same-expiry GOLDTEN / GOLDGUINEA / GOLDPETAL cycle from Apr 2025 to Sep 2026 (about 18 cycles), plus GOLDGUINEA vs GOLDPETAL back to 2020 as context.
  • Re-measure on four marks: Close, Open, (H+L)/2 and VWAP = Value × 10⁵ ÷ (Volume × lot grams). Value is ₹ lakh, verified on real rows. VWAP ignores last-trade timing, so it directly tests the Close artefact. Polled-spot basis was dropped as a mark because GOLDTEN and GOLDGUINEA share the same poll, so it cancels; it stays a GOLDPETAL-only diagnostic.
  • Exclude: the last 5 trading days, zero-OHLC days, and days with either leg under ₹1 Cr turnover. A cycle survives with ≥10 eligible days.
  • Statistics: cluster bootstrap that resamples whole cycles; pooled mean = mean of cycle means.
OutcomeCriteriaWhat we pitch
GO≥8 surviving cycles and, on every mark: pooled ≥ +15 bp, cluster-bootstrap 95% CI above 0, and cycle hit rate ≥75%The premium is the headline: who pays, how much, when it closes (passing pairs only).
PARTIALFewer than 8 surviving cycles (whatever the CI says), or not GO but the VWAP and Close CIs are both above 0One finding inside the decomposition. Not the headline.
NO-GOAnything elseFallback, agreed now: “contract-fairness desk”. The decomposition, each contract’s basis to its own settlement price, and the chooser. Headline: “we tested the apparent small-lot premium against four price marks and the exchange’s own settlement prices, and it doesn’t hold.” The polled-spot angle and the chooser still differentiate us.

  • Close-timing artefact ruled out: VWAP agrees with Close to within 1 bp. The result holds under every sensitivity run (turnover floors, a 3-day tender window, excluding the Jan–Mar 2026 crash cycles).
  • Time-varying: cycle means run from 34 to 145 bp, peaking in the Mar–Jun 2026 cycles.
  • No decay before delivery: traded prices still carry it on expiry day. Only the final settlement price, the same per gram for all three, removes it. The two-cycle “decay” is withdrawn.
  • Rupee view: at about ₹14,800/g the premium is about ₹117–139/g, or about ₹1,170–1,390 per 10 g, lost by anyone holding into settlement or delivery. Exit before tender and you get most of it back: your cost is the premium’s change, about 6–11 bp on average, more when the regime shifts.
  • Also confirmed: gold-rv’s GOLDPETAL break. Against GOLDGUINEA it went from −250 bp (late 2024) to about +30 bp (mid-2025), across the Nov 2024 → Apr 2025 cycles. The cause is still an S1 hypothesis; the sign is awkward for the making-charge story.

MVP: due 31 Oct

M1

Validated data layer

  • Chrome-TLS fetcher.
  • Contract-wise backfill 2020→, plus polled spot. This also feeds G0.
  • Versioned spec YAML.
  • Guards: holiday, date echo, FUTCOM filter, zero-OHLC no-trade days.
  • A committed Parquet snapshot plus a one-command refresh script. The GitHub Actions cron only if time permits; otherwise it moves to the finale (S6).
M2

Basis Anatomy

  • Per contract per day: waterfall from raw price through steps 1–5 above.
  • Basis to own polled spot.
  • Implied carry checked against T-bill rates.
M3

Same-reference convergence monitor

  • GOLDTEN vs GOLDGUINEA vs GOLDPETAL (identical settlement price per gram) across every cycle since Apr 2025.
  • GOLD vs GOLDM as the control pair (same 3-day average settlement; the premium should be about 0). It works only on the near even-month contract: GOLD lists Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct and Dec only, so about 6 cycles a year. On 9 Oct the shared Dec contract was at −2.6 bp; the far shared months barely trade.
M4

Walk-forward verdict

  • Premium rule: test whether any pre-tender version of the premium trade survives measured costs. Given the flat profile, a null is likely, and that is a valid answer.
  • t+1 fills, fine-gram lot matching, EDGE + statutory costs.
  • Gold-leg vs spread-leg P&L split.
  • Random-entry null, block-bootstrap CI, Deflated Sharpe with the honest trial count.
  • Results with and without the Jan–Mar 2026 crash; truncation test in CI.
M5

Trader-facing layer

  • “Which contract?” chooser (keeps the named-user argument): hold X g for N days → ranked effective cost.
  • Cost includes premium, carry, statutory fees, and brokerage. The broker is a parameter; the default preset is labelled “Zerodha rates” (0.03% or ₹20 per order, whichever is lower), so quantity matters.
  • A take-delivery line: making charge (₹100/g on GOLDPETAL coins) and tender-period margin.
  • Shows the tender and broker exit deadlines.
  • In-app alert log, quiet by default, with the reason each day was silent. Replaces Telegram.
  • Contract calendar.

The site is static by design, as gold-rv chose: nothing breaks on stage. A small serverless function is needed only if we ship S3 (the LLM key stays on the server). Python 3.12 on Windows laptops; no paid services.

What makes it novel
  • Settlement-reference basis: the first PS03 build to use MCX’s per-contract polled spot.
  • Contract design as data: versioned specs and circular events inside the decomposition.
  • A finding, not a dashboard (G0: GO): small-lot contracts trade +79 to +94 bp over GOLDTEN in all 18 cycles, and only settlement removes it. The question shifts from “is there arbitrage?” to “who pays, how much, and how to avoid paying it?”
  • Measured costs instead of assumed ones.
  • A named user and decision that stay valuable even if the verdict is “no edge”.
Data sources for the build
